## Trim painting that makes the whole room look new

You can paint four walls and still have a room that looks tired, because the baseboards are scuffed, the door frame is chipped and the trim has turned yellow. Trim painting fixes that. Fresh baseboards, door frames and moldings are like a good haircut for a room: same face, suddenly much sharper.

We sand, fill and caulk first, then finish in a durable paint with clean lines where the trim meets the wall.

## Baseboard painting

Baseboards take the most abuse in any room: vacuums, shoes, chairs, pets, and the occasional kid on a scooter. Baseboard painting starts with cleaning off years of grime, sanding rough spots and filling dents. Then we caulk the gap between the baseboard and the wall, so the line looks continuous instead of cracked.

One thing we won't do: smear caulk over everything and call it a day. Small gaps get caulked. Large gaps, damaged trim and poorly fitted joints get properly repaired. Caulk is a finishing touch, not a magic eraser.

## Interior door painting

Doors get grabbed, bumped and slammed, so they need a tough finish. We paint both sides and all the edges, protect the hinges and hardware, and use a satin or semi-gloss that wipes clean. Paneled doors take more time than flat ones, because every groove gets brushed by hand.

**What affects the cost of interior door painting:**

- The number of doors and frames
- Flat vs. paneled doors
- Their condition: chips, dents and old drips
- Color change: dark to light, or the other way around, can need extra primer

We price it all as one flat-rate quote.

## Why old trim takes longer

Prewar NYC apartments often have beautiful original trim under decades of paint layers. Some baseboards have so many coats they've practically become a historical record. That build-up hides the detail and leaves drips, chips and soft edges. Old trim can take longer than the walls, and it's worth it: sanding it smooth and recaulking brings back the crisp lines it was built with.

## The right paint for trim and doors

Trim and doors need a finish that's tougher than your walls. We use satin or semi-gloss, depending on the look you want and how much wear the surface gets. Glossier finishes are tougher and easier to clean, but they also show every flaw underneath, which is one more reason the prep matters.

Two full coats, every time. If you're asking whether trim needs a second coat, it needs a second coat.
