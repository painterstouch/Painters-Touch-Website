## Ceiling painting done right the first time

The ceiling is the biggest surface in most rooms that nobody looks at, until it's yellowed, cracked or stained. Then it's all anyone can see. Ceiling painting is also the job most people least want to do themselves: neck craned, paint dripping toward your face, and a roller that seems to get heavier every minute. Ceilings are not for the faint of arm.

That's where our ceiling painters come in. We protect the room, fix what needs fixing, and leave you with an even, fresh ceiling and sharp lines where it meets the walls.

## Why we paint the ceiling first

On every job, the order is ceiling first, then walls, then trim and doors. Gravity always wins, so we paint from the top down. That way, any drip lands on a surface that hasn't been finished yet, not on your fresh walls.

## Cut lines that are actually straight

In older NYC buildings, ceilings are rarely perfectly level, and the line between ceiling and wall can wander. Painter's tape can't follow a wavy line, so we cut the ceiling line by hand with a brush. It takes longer, and it's the difference between a room that looks "painted" and one that looks finished.

## Water stains and cracks

A brown ring on the ceiling usually means water got in at some point. Painting right over it doesn't work: the stain bleeds back through, sometimes within days. Here's how we handle it:

1. **The leak has to be fixed first,** and the area has to be fully dry.
2. **We seal the stain** with a stain-blocking primer.
3. **Then two full coats** of ceiling paint, blended into the rest of the ceiling.

Hairline cracks get filled, sanded and primed. A crack that keeps coming back can be a sign of movement that needs a closer look before painting, and we'll tell you honestly if that's the case.

## High ceilings, prewar ceilings and stairwells

Ten-foot prewar ceilings are beautiful. They're also tall (we measured). Brownstone stair halls and two-story foyers on Long Island are even taller. We bring the right ladders or scaffolding, protect the stairs and floors, and include all of it in your flat-rate price.

## Bathroom and kitchen ceilings

Bathrooms and kitchens are where ceilings work hardest: steam, splashes and cooking all leave their mark. We pick a finish that can handle moisture and wipes clean, and we take extra care around vents, lights and fixtures. One of our customers, Krysten, had us repaint her entire apartment and wrote that we "helped us out with tricky bathroom ceilings as well." Tricky ceilings are kind of our thing.

## What ceiling painting costs

Every ceiling is different, so we price each job as one written, flat-rate quote. What moves the price:

- **Size and height** of the ceiling
- **Condition:** stains, cracks, peeling paint or old patches
- **Color change:** going from a dark or stained ceiling to bright white can take extra primer
- **Bundling:** painting the ceiling with the walls is usually more efficient than painting it alone
